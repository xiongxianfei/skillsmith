# View definitions

Each view is one angle on the city, defined by the question it answers. The same item can appear in several views by ID.

| View | Definition | Question | Built from | Picture |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Place | Sights and locations worth visiting, ranked by importance for the trip | What is there? | Places | Cards, checklist |
| Taste | Dishes, drinks, sweets and fruit that represent the city, with where and when to try them | What do I eat and drink? | Food | Food board, checklist |
| Culture | Main themes (history, trade, crafts, traditions) that explain why places and food matter | Why does it matter? | Culture, linked to Places and Food | Network diagram |
| Space | Geography: where things are, how they cluster, how to move between them | Where is it? | Places (Area, Latitude, Longitude) | Tourist map |
| Time | Timing: best months, seasons, weather, best time of day per item | When should I go? | Weather, Season, Best time | Month strip, chart |
| Scenarios | Example trips combining the other views into a usable plan | How do I use it all? | Scenarios | Timeline, day cards |

How they differ: Place and Taste say what exists. Culture says why it matters. Space and Time say where and when. Scenarios tie everything into a plan and test whether the other views are complete.

Choosing views for a new kind of city or purpose: list the questions people ask, group them into non-overlapping views, keep each view to one concern, and test with scenarios.
