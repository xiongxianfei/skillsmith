# Prescription, lens selection and actual frame fit

This reference governs **boundaries**, not clinical diagnosis or prescribing. A visually flattering frame may be clinically or mechanically unsuitable; an optician must verify actual fit and dispensing measurements.

## Prescription data fields

- `OD` / `OS`: right eye / left eye (verify eye order when the source does not specify).
- `SPH`: spherical lens correction; sign (+/−) is essential.
- `CYL`: astigmatism correction; sign and axis must be transcribed exactly.
- `AXIS`: cylindrical orientation, often 1–180°. Do not infer axis from a photograph.
- `ADD`, prism, base direction: relevant where present; do not fabricate.
- `PD`: pupillary distance; distinguish **binocular distance PD** from **monocular right/left PD** and **near PD**. Don't silently replace one with another.

Never assume that a machine/autorefractor readout represents the **subjectively confirmed final spectacle prescription**. Values with unusual decimals may be machine readings. Clarify if the user intends to order lenses; comparison of frame styles can proceed without resolved Rx.

## Geometry and dispensing

- Frame marking `A–DBL–temple` generally denotes lens width, distance-between-lenses (bridge), and temple length in millimetres. These numbers are a starting point, not proof of fit.
- The sum of monocular PDs can give binocular PD for the *same measurement*, but does not uniquely determine lens width, bridge size, overall frame width, pupil fitting height, face form, or nose geometry.
- Effective diameter, frame shape, prescription, centring, optical centre position and lens design affect final edge thickness. With minus lenses, larger effective diameter and decentration can increase edge thickness; this is a tendency, not an exact calculator from photos.
- A 1.60 vs 1.67 vs 1.74 index choice should be evaluated with the optician in the actual frame. Higher index can reduce thickness in some circumstances but has cost/optical tradeoffs.
- Full-rim acetate can visually conceal edges, while slim frames may feel lighter; actual comfort depends on total mass and adjustments.

## Physical fitting checklist

- Eyes sit sensibly within the lens aperture and the brow line does not awkwardly obscure vision.
- Frame rests securely on the nose without slipping or painful pressure.
- Temples rest comfortably without squeezing the head or ear; glasses do not touch the cheeks or eyelashes.
- Frame remains aligned when the person looks down or moves their head.
- Check frame with normal haircut and daily clothing, from front, both obliques and one true side profile.
- Optician verifies individual optical centres, appropriate heights, fit, prescriptions, lens material/coatings, and safe mounting.

## Vision change precaution

For reported worsening myopia or astigmatism, advise an up-to-date comprehensive eye examination before new lenses; do not conclude progression from incomparable machine and final Rx numbers. Changing or irregular astigmatism may warrant assessment of corneal shape at the optometrist's discretion. Sudden loss of vision, severe eye pain, flashes/curtain, or other acute symptoms should receive urgent medical assessment.

No AI preview can validate sharpness, distortion, binocular function, refraction or real lens comfort. State that clearly without turning an ordinary style query into a medical consultation.
