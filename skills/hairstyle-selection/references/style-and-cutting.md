# Haircut taxonomy and barber-language reference

## Keep three decisions separate

1. **Haircut geometry:** top/fringe length, layers, weight line, connected/disconnected sides, taper height and neckline.
2. **Texture and hair behaviour:** naturally straight/wavy/curly, forward growth, cowlicks, air-dried fullness, and optional chemical texture treatment.
3. **Styling finish:** polished side part, loose separated texture, pushed-back look, soft curtains, matte vs glossy, heat/products and maintenance.

Two polished images can be the same **cut with different styling**, or two very different **cuts with similar styling**. Explain which dimension actually differs.

## Useful style families (examples, not a ranking)

| Family | Cut distinction | Styling distinction | Common trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Textured crop | Shorter top with forward fringe | Irregular, broken-up fringe | Heavy fringe can mask forehead |
| Ivy League | Short top, longer front, tapered sides | Lift/comb to side | Needs control for forward-growing front |
| Classic taper with brushed-back top | Tapered sides connected to medium top | Neat backward direction | Daily drying may be required |
| Side part / side sweep | Medium top, connected sides | Defined part vs loose diagonal sweep | Fringe needs sufficient length |
| Soft curtains | Medium layered top, parted fringe | 5:5 or 6:4 separation | Bulk at temples, fringe at glasses |
| Medium-long flow | Longer connected layers | Swept back/side or loose fall | More weight at ears and nape |
| Layered shag | Varied long layers/texture | Airy separated movement | Shape and maintenance more specific |

These labels describe families rather than standardised centimetre prescriptions. Adjust cut and products to individual hair type and stated preferences.

## Fade vs taper vs undercut

- **Scissor-led natural taper:** smoothly reduces length toward ear/nape; keeps upper sides connected and covered; scissors may be assisted by clippers at the edges.
- **Low clipper taper:** controlled short transition concentrated low around ears/sideburns/nape; may or may not reveal scalp depending on guard length.
- **Skin fade:** blend down to bare skin. A *low* skin fade can still reveal scalp. A low taper need not be a skin fade.
- **High fade:** short blending extends high up the side of the head; not interchangeable with a low taper.
- **Disconnected undercut:** noticeable abrupt length jump from long top to short side. It can exist with or without skin exposure.
- **Scissor-over-comb:** blending/weight removal technique, not a named haircut.

Do not universally prohibit a fade: the user's aesthetic and barber capability govern the choice. When the user requests full upper sides, explicitly forbid a high fade, skin fade, and disconnected top as *their preferences*.

## Barber brief template

**Target look:** [style name, polished/loose finish, parting].

**Cut structure (starting guidance; adapt on dry hair):**

- Front/fringe: [keep enough reach for preferred movement; provisional length if useful].
- Top: [medium/short/long, layer type and controlled weight].
- Crown: [whorl position and direction; avoid root over-thinning when relevant].
- Upper sides: [scissor blend, connection to top, desired fullness].
- Temples/ears/sideburns: [taper height; glasses clearance].
- Back/nape: [weight, outline, neckline, low taper if chosen].
- Avoid: [specific mistakes linked to user's priorities].

**Styling finish:** [root drying direction, part, light product, degree of texture].

**Verification request:** Have barber show the side and back shape after cutting, demonstrate the styling, and check the finished cut with/without glasses if relevant.

Do not turn AI imagery into millimetre-precise guards. Where an approximate range is provided (e.g. fringe around 8–11 cm), mark it a discussion starting point, not a literal measurement from a generated image.
