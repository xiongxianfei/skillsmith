# Photography and view labelling for hairstyles

Use this reference when the user requests photographs, multi-angle assessment, or multi-view barber imagery. The seven-view arrangement below is a **working convention**, not a universally codified barbershop standard. Angles indicate where the camera is placed relative to an upright, stationary subject, with `right/left` always the **subject's anatomical right/left**, not the viewer's.

| Label | Working azimuth | Camera placement | Reveals |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 01_frontal | 0° | Directly in front | Fringe, face framing, overall width |
| 02_right_anterior_oblique | +45° | Right-front of subject | Right temple transition, flow and volume |
| 03_right_lateral_profile | +90° | Subject's right | Right ear, side length, taper height |
| 04_posterior | 180° | Directly behind | Crown, occiput, nape shape |
| 05_left_lateral_profile | −90° | Subject's left | Left ear, side length, taper height |
| 06_left_anterior_oblique | −45° | Left-front of subject | Left temple transition, flow and volume |
| 07_superior | top-down (azimuth N/A) | Above upright head, looking vertically down | Natural part, coverage, crown whorl |

**Angle definition:** Positive horizontal azimuth here means the camera moves toward the subject's right. A 90° lateral profile should visibly show the named ear. The superior view is a different **camera elevation**, not a 180° rear view. A head bowed toward a front camera is only an approximate top-down view.

## Capture protocol

- Obtain the subject's permission for the intended processing. Prefer newly taken portraits against a blank wall, without location clues or other people.
- Use a rear camera at approximately eye level and about 1.5–2 metres away (adjust based on lens). Keep zoom/focal length and framing comparable. Avoid ultrawide close selfies, beauty filters and strong portrait blur around the hairline.
- Keep the head upright and face at a neutral expression; preferably move the camera around the subject instead of repeatedly turning/tilting the head.
- Keep the entire hair silhouette, temples, ears, nape and upper shoulders in frame. For superior view, raise the camera over an upright head or have someone hold it securely overhead.
- Take natural air-dried, unstyled hair first when diagnosing *hair behaviour*; if desired, take a second styled set, clearly labelled. Record whether combing, heat, styling products or chemical treatment were used.
- If glasses are routinely worn, photograph at least one oblique angle with glasses to check side volume where arms rest.
- Use neutral filenames without names, birthdates or location; strip unnecessary EXIF if the user can do so safely. This does not remove facial identifiability.

## Image-quality and naming checks

- Confirm subject-relative right and left by the **visible ear**, not by mirrored phone previews. Flag possible accidental mirroring.
- Confirm each frame is a genuine angle rather than an AI-invented reconstruction. Generated rear and overhead views are **illustrative only** if original photos do not exist.
- Do not infer centimetres of hair length or head-to-face ratios from photos alone.
- If some of the seven views are absent, proceed with what is available and say where the resulting recommendation is less confident; never demand the complete set by default.
