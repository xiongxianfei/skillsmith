# Hairstyle preview production, prompts and delivery

## Controlled preview production

1. Confirm authorised use of portraits. If previewing an actual person, edit a real source photo rather than inventing a new face from a description, where the tool supports it.
2. Choose a locked **anchor portrait**: neutral face, clear haircut outline, similar lighting, one fixed angle. Log whether hair is natural or styled.
3. Generate one candidate style at a time. Request a **change only to hair**; retain face proportions, hairline where possible, ears, eyes, lighting, clothing and background.
4. Reject any preview that changes facial identity/features, introduces an implausible whorl, contradicts the cut structure, or alters the intended side taper.
5. Select genuinely different finalist cuts on the same anchor. Use consistent zoom and view. Add captions **after generation** (not baked into the photograph) to reduce garbled labels.
6. Only after shortlist agreement, create right/left oblique, side, posterior and superior barber views as justified. Use real source angle photos for each view if possible. Generated missing angles are artistic extrapolations.

## Reusable image prompts

### Comparison portrait

> Edit the supplied **authorised** portrait into a photorealistic hairstyle preview. Change only the hair to [cut family, fringe, part, top layers, upper sides, taper, nape, styling]. Preserve facial structure, expression, skin texture, ears, clothes, background, lighting and camera angle. Do not beautify, slim or reshape the face. Do not add captions, clothing changes or other objects. Show an achievable cut for [natural hair observations]. The result is a concept, not an exact real-world prediction.

### Side profile for barber

> Edit the authentic [subject-right / subject-left] lateral-profile photo. Keep the same person's face, ear, angle and lighting. Show the agreed [cut] with an unbroken transition between upper sides and top, [requested taper height], realistic sideburn coverage and [back/nape]. Preserve the stated hair growth pattern. Avoid [user-specific non-goals]. Do not invent visible skin fade or disconnected cut when the agreed style requires full scissor blending.

### Posterior / superior

> Use the original posterior [or directly overhead] source photograph of the same person as the base. Show the approved haircut with [crown and nape specification], preserving real head geometry and whorl orientation as much as possible. Mark any details that cannot be verified from the source as illustrative. No fake numerical measurements.

## Two-part barber deliverable

**A. Hero look:** One large, clear front or 45° image of the *preferred styling*. Label the finished aesthetic separately from cutting directions.

**B. Technical sheet:** Real or source-grounded right/left 90° side profiles, posterior view, and where relevant a top view, plus short verified callouts on upper-side length, taper height, ear clearance, crown continuity and nape. For a fade-focused barber and a no-short-sides preference, prominently write `KEEP UPPER SIDES CONNECTED AND FULLER; LOW TAPER ONLY; DO NOT SHAVE HIGH`.

## Acceptance checklist

- [ ] All panels depict one coherent cut, not a different haircut in each view.
- [ ] Frontal, right, left, posterior and superior labels match what is visibly shown.
- [ ] Same fringe length and part direction across comparable angles.
- [ ] Upper-side fullness, sideburn taper height and crown/nape shape consistent across angles.
- [ ] The user-preferred finishing style is clearly the *primary* reference when an older technical reference differs.
- [ ] No annotations treated as photographic measurements or guaranteed clipper guard sizes.
- [ ] The barber has the original unstyled source photos when they are necessary to judge natural growth.
- [ ] Generated views labelled as visual targets; physical cut assessed after washing and ordinary home styling.
